# Ahead of Main

The brand identity for the public developer brief formerly published as DEV Ahead, at
[aheadofmain.com](https://aheadofmain.com). The format rules for each issue live in
[docs/editorial-guide.md](docs/editorial-guide.md); this file covers what the brand is and how it
sounds. Where the two overlap, the editorial guide wins for issue format and this file wins for
voice, naming and claims.

## Strategy

### Overview

Ahead of Main is a twice-weekly brief for working developers about what's moving underneath
developer news: early signals, capabilities you can adopt this week, and a running Disruption Watch
board that rates how far each shift has got.

It began as a private brief, written for one developer, about the tooling and AI changes that
would matter to their work before they reached the headlines. The public edition keeps the method
and drops everything personal.

What it really does is close the gap between when a change becomes visible (in a changelog, a
paper, a pricing page, a default that quietly flipped) and when most developers hear about it. In
git terms, it's the branch that's a few commits ahead: the work is already there, it just hasn't
been merged into the mainstream yet.

The problem isn't a shortage of news. It's that the shifts that change how developers work show
up as small, scattered, low-drama evidence, and news coverage rewards the opposite. By the time a
shift is a headline, the people who noticed early have already adjusted.

Before: you find out a default changed when your bill or your build changes. After: you saw the
changelog line, knew how sure to be about it, and tried it on a Monday.

Long-term ambition: to be the brief working developers trust to tell them, honestly and early,
which shifts are real.

### Audience

Primary audience: working developers and tech leads who already read the news and ship software
for a living. They use AI coding tools daily or are being asked to, they make tooling and vendor
decisions for a team or for clients, and they need to know which changes deserve an afternoon and
which can wait.

They already believe most AI and dev-tool coverage is hype, and that the real changes are in
changelogs, pricing pages and defaults. They're skeptical of benchmark claims, of "this changes
everything", of newsletters that are link dumps, and of anyone who sounds smarter than the
evidence allows.

Secondary audience: the editor's own team and LinkedIn network, who read it to keep up and to
share. They need the same honesty, but they arrive one issue at a time and without context, so
each issue has to stand on its own.

Not for: beginners looking for tutorials, readers who want a complete list of everything that
shipped, investors looking for stock or market calls, and anyone who wants their hype confirmed.

### Positioning

Category: an early-signal brief for working developers.

Not a news roundup. Not a link digest. Not an AI hype feed. Not a research newsletter. Not a
tutorial site.

The market has three layers. Link digests (TLDR, Bytes, JavaScript Weekly) report what shipped,
quickly and broadly. Analysis newsletters (The Pragmatic Engineer, ByteByteGo) explain how the
industry and its systems work, in depth and at a slower pace. AI research newsletters (Ahead of
AI, Import AI) follow papers and models. Ahead of Main sits between them: it reads the same
primary sources as the digests, ranks them the way an analyst would, and follows each shift over
time, the way none of them do.

Structural differentials:
- The Disruption Watch board carries ratings from issue to issue, on a four-rung ladder
  (Watching, Accelerating, Hitting the market, Repricing the work), and a rating only moves when
  there's specific evidence.
- Every item carries a tag (ACT NOW, WATCH, CONTEXT), and every early signal carries a confidence
  level, so the reader knows how sure to be.
- Sources are linked at the original page; items from a secondary digest are marked unverified,
  and vendor numbers are attributed to the vendor.
- Every issue ends with an experiment someone can build in 30 to 90 minutes, with a clear "done
  when".
- The whole thing lives in a public repository, so anyone can challenge a rating with an issue.

Territory: the gap between "it's in the changelog" and "it's in the headlines". Ahead of Main
owns *early, with evidence*.

### Personality

Archetype: The Lookout / The Forecaster. Someone who walks a little ahead of the group, reports
back what's coming, and says how sure they are.

Attributes: Early, Evidenced, Practical, Candid, Calm, Open.

What it IS: Early. Plain. Sourced. Useful by Monday.

What it is NOT: Not hype. Not smug. Not breathless. Not doom. Not gatekeeping. Not a thought
leader.

### References & Anti-References

#### References

- **A good changelog:** Borrow its terseness, version numbers, and refusal to editorialise a
  fact. Do not borrow its lack of context; every item here must say why it matters.
- **A weather forecast:** Borrow the habit of stating confidence plainly ("70% chance") and of
  being judged on whether it was right, not on how exciting it sounded. Do not borrow the
  false precision of a number nobody measured.
- **The Economist's briefings:** Borrow the ranked, opinionated judgement and the confidence to
  say what matters most this week. Do not borrow the unsigned, all-knowing tone; Ahead of Main
  says when it couldn't confirm something.
- **Simon Willison's weblog:** Borrow the hands-on habit of trying the thing and linking every
  source. Do not borrow the personal-diary register; the brief has a byline but no personal
  stories.

#### Anti-References

- **AI hype threads on X and LinkedIn:** Reject "this changes everything", 🤯, "10 tools you're
  missing", and any framing that makes the reader feel behind.
- **Link-dump digests:** Reject long unranked lists where every item weighs the same and none
  says why it matters.
- **Thought-leader posts:** Reject invented anecdotes ("I've watched teams…"), war stories that
  can't be checked, and opinions dressed up as findings.

### Promise

Core promise:
- You hear about a shift before it's a headline.
- You know how sure to be, and where it came from.
- You get something you can try this week.

Base message: Ahead of Main reads the changelogs, papers and pricing pages twice a week and tells
working developers which shifts are real, how sure to be, and what to try, before the shift is
merged into the mainstream.

Synthesizing phrase: A few commits ahead, never a head above.

### Guardrails

Tone summary: Plain, early, evidenced, calm.

What the brand cannot be:
- A hype brand
- A brand that makes readers feel behind or less capable
- A "better than other developers" brand; the edge is *earlier*, never *smarter*
- A personal-brand vehicle built on invented experience
- A source of investment, market or career predictions

Litmus test: If it would make a careful reader feel behind, or make Ahead of Main sound surer
than its sources, it's wrong.

## Voice

### Identity

Ahead of Main is for developers who already read the news and want to know what's coming next.
I read the changelogs, release notes, papers and pricing pages that most coverage skips, pick the
few changes that will affect how you work, and say plainly how sure I am about each one.

I'm not here to tell you everything that shipped, and I'm not here to tell you the sky is falling.
Most weeks, a handful of things matter. Some weeks, nothing does, and I'll say so rather than pad
the issue.

Every rating on the Disruption Watch board stays until the evidence moves it, every source is
linked, and anything I couldn't confirm is marked. If you think I've got one wrong, open an issue
on the repo.

Essence: Early, with evidence.

### Tagline & Slogans

Primary: **"What main hasn't merged yet."** Use it on the site header, the about page, the
LinkedIn newsletter description and the dev.to profile. It works for developers who read it as
git and for anyone who reads "main" as "mainstream".

Alternatives:
- "A few commits ahead."
- "Early, with evidence."
- "What's moving underneath developer news, twice a week." (the descriptive line, kept from DEV
  Ahead, for places that need a plain explanation)

Slogans by context:
- Site header: "What main hasn't merged yet."
- RSS and dev.to description: "Early signals for working developers, twice a week."

### Message Pillars

**Early**
- The shifts that matter show up in changelogs and defaults before they show up in headlines.
- Reading the primary source is how you get there first.

**Evidenced**
- Every item is linked to the page it came from.
- If it couldn't be confirmed, it says so.

**Calibrated**
- Every signal says how sure to be.
- A rating moves only when specific evidence moves it.

**Practical**
- Every issue ends with something you can try this week, with a time cost.
- "Why it matters" means what changes for you on Monday.

**Open**
- The brief, the board and its history are public.
- Anyone can challenge a rating.

### Tonal Rules

- Lead with what changed, in one plain sentence, then say why it matters.
- Name the mechanism, not the benefit: "fork subagents are now on by default in `claude -p`",
  not "a big upgrade for automation".
- State confidence every time something is uncertain, and say what would change your mind.
- Attribute vendor numbers to the vendor: "Meta's own benchmarks", "Microsoft's figures".
- Say plainly when a window was quiet. Cut before you pad.
- Use "you" for the reader. Use "I" only for the editor's judgement or limits ("I couldn't
  confirm the terms"). Never "we".
- Never invent personal experience, anecdotes or team stories.
- Never make the reader feel behind. Frame the edge as time, not talent.
- No exclamation marks, no emoji in body copy, no "game-changer", no "revolutionary".
- Numbers only when a source states them, with the source named.
- When something is a limitation of the brief (a paper not read at source, a claim from a
  secondary digest), say so in the item.
- Opinions are welcome; label them as opinion and keep them short.

Identity boundaries:
- I am not a news roundup.
- I am not a hype account.
- I am not an investment or market forecaster.
- I am not a tutorial site.
- I am not smarter than the reader, just a few commits ahead.

| We Say | We Never Say |
|---|---|
| "It's in the changelog, not the headlines yet" | "Breaking: this changes everything" |
| "Medium confidence; one source" | "Everyone's talking about…" |
| "Meta's own benchmarks" | "The best model ever made" |
| "Try it this week; about an hour" | "10 tools you're missing out on" |
| "A quiet window" | "Another huge week in AI" |
| "A few commits ahead" | "Stay ahead of other developers" |
| "I couldn't confirm the terms" | "Sources say" |

## Visual

### Logo & Marks

Primary mark: the wordmark "Ahead of Main" set in Schibsted Grotesk on Paper, with "Ahead of" in
Ink and "Main" in Cobalt. "of" is never dropped or replaced with a symbol. A one-colour version
in Ink is used wherever Cobalt isn't available.

Interim symbol: the current favicon, four rising bars in the Disruption Watch rung colours on an
Ink square, is carried over from DEV Ahead. It represents the board, not the name, and a symbol
for Ahead of Main has not been designed yet.

Clear space: at least the height of the capital "A" on every side of the wordmark.

Prohibitions: never stretch or rotate the wordmark, never set it in a gradient, never recolour it
outside the Core Colors, never add a git-branch icon as decoration.

Source files: [public/favicon.svg](public/favicon.svg).

### Core Colors

These are the colours already live on the site, carried over to Ahead of Main unchanged.

- **Ink** `#16202A` (mandatory). The voice of the brief: a blue-black that reads like printed
  type, never pure black.
- **Paper** `#F6F7F5` (mandatory). A cool, quiet ground, so the page feels like a briefing sheet
  rather than an app.
- **Cobalt** `#2B59D1` (mandatory). The thread to follow: sources, references, the way to the
  original page. Used sparingly, so it stays meaningful.

The Disruption Watch rung colours are a data encoding of the ladder, not identity colours, and
belong in a DESIGN.md.

Avoid: purple-to-blue gradients, neon green "terminal" palettes, and glow effects. They belong to
AI-hype and hacker-aesthetic brands, which is exactly what this brand rejects.

### Typefaces

- **Source Serif 4**, the voice of the brief: issue text and anything the reader reads at
  length. Chosen on 2026-10-26 to replace Newsreader, for a crisper, more technical reading
  voice. Fallback: `'Iowan Old Style', Georgia, serif`. Licensing: SIL Open Font License 1.1
  (Adobe, via Google Fonts).
- **Schibsted Grotesk**, for the wordmark, headings, labels, tags and metadata. Fallback:
  `ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif`. Licensing: SIL Open Font License 1.1 (Schibsted, via
  Google Fonts). Verified 2026-10-26.
- **System monospace**, for code, commands, version numbers and environment variables only.
  Fallback: `ui-monospace, 'SF Mono', Menlo, monospace`. No licence needed; it uses the
  reader's own fonts.

The split is semantic: the serif carries the editor's judgement, the grotesk carries structure,
and the monospace carries anything you'd paste into a terminal.

### Photography & Illustration

Mood: quiet, factual, unhurried.

Subjects: none, by default. Issues carry no photography.

Avoid: stock photos, glowing brains, robot hands, people pointing at screens, AI-generated hero
images.

Illustration: only charts and diagrams that explain a mechanism or show the board's ratings over
time. Never decorative.

### Art Direction

Design keywords: Briefing sheet. One ink. Dated header. Source lines. Confidence marks. Hairline
rules. Calm paper.

Direction statement: The identity should look like a forecast someone checks every Monday, not a
feed someone scrolls. Every element should help the reader judge what's true and what to do.

Visual territory: **the morning briefing sheet in a forecasting office.** One ink on pale paper.
A dated header with the window it covers. Each item is a short block with a plain headline, a
"why it matters" line and a source line at the foot. A small confidence mark sits next to
anything uncertain. One blue marks the threads to follow. A single chart, the ladder, is the
thing everyone looks at first. Nothing glows, nothing animates for attention, and nothing is
there for decoration.

## Governance

### Naming

- The name is always "Ahead of Main": three words, title case, with a lower-case "of". Never
  "AheadOfMain", "AOM" or "Ahead" on its own in public copy.
- `aheadofmain` (all lower case, one word) is used only for the domain and account handles:
  aheadofmain.com, dev.to, GitHub, LinkedIn.
- Issues are numbered with no gaps and titled with three short clauses naming the three biggest
  things, as in the editorial guide.
- Section names in an issue are fixed by the editorial guide. New sections need the editor's
  approval and an update to that guide.
- The four rung names are reserved and never reworded.
- "DEV Ahead" is the retired public name. "DEV Digest" is the private feeder and never appears in
  public.
- Will not use: "Ahead of AI" or anything that echoes it (it's Sebastian Raschka's newsletter),
  "ahead of the curve", "alpha", "insider", "10x".
- The byline is the handle SiyadDev. The editor's real name never appears.

### Claims

Approved:
- "Twice a week." Evidence: the routine runs twice weekly, and issues are dated 12, 15, 19 and 22
  October 2026 in [src/content/issues](src/content/issues).
- "Every issue lives in a public GitHub repository." Evidence: the public repository
  SiyadDev/dev-ahead.
- "Researched with an AI agent, edited by a person before it's published." Evidence: the about
  page discloses it, each issue arrives as a pull request from the routine, and the branch
  ruleset on `main` requires a pull request before anything is published.

Draft, not yet approved:
- "Every item links its source." The editorial guide requires it, but `npm run check` doesn't
  enforce it. It needs either a check or an audit of past issues.

Requires the editor's review before use: any subscriber or reader count, any statement that
Ahead of Main reported something first, any comparison that names another newsletter.

Prohibited:
- "Ahead of other developers", "the developers who win", or any claim that readers are better
  than non-readers.
- Any time-saving or productivity percentage. None has been measured.
- Any track-record or accuracy claim for the Disruption Watch until the board has history to
  show it.
- Any investment, market or job-security advice.

An agent may draft a candidate claim, but it stays labelled draft until the editor approves it
and attaches evidence.
